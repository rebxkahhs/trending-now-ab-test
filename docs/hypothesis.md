# Experiment Hypothesis: Trending Now Product Card Callout

## Business Context

An e-commerce retailer is evaluating whether a "Trending Now" callout displayed on product cards can increase customer engagement and accelerate product sales.

The business currently has products where increased visibility may help improve inventory movement. The proposed intervention is a visual merchandising change: displaying a "Trending Now" badge on eligible product cards to create additional social proof and encourage customers to consider those products.

The goal of this experiment is to determine whether the callout changes customer purchasing behavior enough to justify broader implementation.

---

## Business Question

Does displaying a "Trending Now" callout on a product card increase the likelihood that a customer adds that product to their cart?

---

## Experiment Design

### Treatment Group

Customers exposed to an eligible product card displaying the "Trending Now" callout.

### Control Group

Customers exposed to the same or comparable eligible product card without the "Trending Now" callout.

### Experimental Unit

Customer-product exposure.

Each observation represents a customer being exposed to a product card during a shopping session.

---

## Primary Metric

### Add-to-Cart Rate

Add-to-cart rate is defined as:

> Number of customers who add the product to their cart / Number of customers exposed to the product card

This metric was selected because it measures the immediate behavioral impact of the product-card intervention while accounting for differences in exposure volume.

---

## Secondary Metrics and Guardrails

The following metrics will be evaluated as supporting measures:

### Purchase Conversion Rate

Measures whether increased add-to-cart behavior translates into completed purchases.

### Product Engagement Rate

Measures whether the callout increases customer interaction with the product experience.

The primary decision will not be based solely on statistical significance. The magnitude of the effect and practical business impact will also be considered.

---

## Hypotheses

### Null Hypothesis (H₀)

There is no difference in add-to-cart rate between customers exposed to product cards with the "Trending Now" callout and customers exposed to product cards without the callout.

### Alternative Hypothesis (H₁)

Customers exposed to product cards with the "Trending Now" callout have a higher add-to-cart rate than customers exposed to product cards without the callout.

---

## Expected Direction of Effect

The expected outcome is that the "Trending Now" callout will increase add-to-cart behavior by providing additional product context and social proof.

However, the analysis will evaluate the result objectively and will not assume the intervention is effective before testing.

---

## Statistical Plan

The primary analysis will compare add-to-cart rates between treatment and control groups.

Planned analysis:

- Estimate treatment and control add-to-cart rates
- Calculate absolute lift and relative lift
- Perform statistical significance testing
- Calculate confidence intervals around the observed difference
- Evaluate whether the observed effect is practically meaningful

The statistical test selection will be finalized after reviewing the data structure and metric distribution.

---

## Planned Segment Analysis

After evaluating the overall treatment effect, the analysis will investigate whether the effect differs across important customer segments:

- New vs. returning customers
- Device type
- Campaign channel
- Product category
- Product price range

Segment analysis will be treated as exploratory and will account for potential sample-size limitations.

---

## Pre-Registration Statement

This hypothesis, metric definition, and analysis plan were documented before conducting statistical testing to reduce the risk of changing evaluation criteria after observing results.
